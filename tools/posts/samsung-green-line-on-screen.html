One day your Samsung looks perfect. The next there's a thin green line running straight down the screen. You restart it and the line is still there. You put it on charge, take the case off, try dark mode, and it still won't go away.

If this has happened to you, you're in good company. A green line (and sometimes a pink, purple or white one) is one of the most common faults we see on Galaxy phones, especially the S series. People often find it in the morning and are sure they didn't do anything to the phone.

This guide explains what that line actually is, why it happens, whether it can be fixed, and why we only fit genuine Samsung service screens on S series phones.

What the green line actually is

Galaxy S phones use an AMOLED display. Unlike older LCD screens, there's no backlight. Every tiny pixel makes its own light. That's why Samsung screens look so sharp and the blacks look so deep.

The flip side is that the display is a very thin, layered panel with fine connections running through it. When one of those connections inside the panel fails, a whole column of pixels can get stuck. That's the line you're seeing.

In plain terms, a permanent vertical green line means part of the display panel has failed. It's a hardware fault inside the screen itself.

Why it happens

We get asked this a lot, especially by people who swear the phone was never dropped. These are the usual causes we see at the bench:

  • A drop or knock. Even a small drop onto a hard floor can stress the panel. The line doesn't always appear straight away. It can show up days or weeks later.
  • Pressure. Sitting down with the phone in a back pocket, or something heavy pressing on it in a bag, can bend the panel slightly.
  • Heat. Leaving the phone in a hot car, on the dashboard or charging under a pillow puts strain on the display.
  • Liquid. Moisture that gets into the phone can attack the display connections over time. See water damage repair if the phone got wet.
  • Age. On some phones, the panel just gives out after a few years of use.

Often there's no single moment you can point to. That's normal. It doesn't change what the fix is.

Signs to look out for

A green line can turn up on its own, but it often comes with other display symptoms. Look for:

  • A thin vertical line that stays in the same spot, whatever is on the screen.
  • The line is visible on the lock screen and in every app.
  • A pink, purple or white line instead of green. It's the same kind of fault.
  • A patch of the screen that won't respond to touch near the line.
  • Areas that flicker, look washed out, or show a green tint at low brightness.
  • Black spots or ink-like blotches spreading from a corner or from a crack.

If the line is in the exact same place no matter what you open, it's in the panel, not in an app.

Can it be repaired?

Yes. The fix is to replace the screen.

A failed display panel isn't something you can repair by changing a setting, doing a factory reset or waiting for it to go away. The line won't come back to normal by itself. In most cases it gets worse over time, and once the screen is replaced, the line is gone for good.

The good news is that the rest of your phone is almost always fine. The cameras, battery, storage and everything else are untouched. You don't need a new phone. You need a new screen.

Why we only use genuine Samsung service screens on the S series

This is where a lot of people get caught out, so it's worth explaining properly.

For iPhones, there are different screen grades to choose from. For the Galaxy S series, there's no worthwhile aftermarket option. The copy screens on the market just don't measure up:

  • Colours and brightness are off. Copies often look dull, too blue or uneven next to the original.
  • The fingerprint reader can stop working. On S series phones the fingerprint sensor sits under the display. Many copy screens make it slow, unreliable or dead.
  • Touch feels different. Lag, missed taps and ghost touches are common.
  • They don't last. We see copy screens come back with the same green line or dead areas much sooner.

That's why we don't fit them. On S series phones, we fit a new genuine Samsung service-pack screen. It's the same screen Samsung uses, so your phone looks and works exactly like it did out of the box, with the right colours, full brightness and working fingerprint unlock.

The service pack comes with the frame

Genuine Samsung service screens come with the frame already fitted. The display is pre-bonded to a new middle frame at the factory. That means:

  • The screen sits perfectly flush, with no gaps or lifting at the edges.
  • The phone gets a fresh frame, so scuffs and dents on the old sides are gone too.
  • The fit and finish match a new phone, not a phone that's been glued back together.

What happens during the repair

  1. Check first. The technician confirms it's the display, and checks the phone for liquid damage or other faults so there are no surprises.
  2. Back up if you can. If the screen still works, back up before you come in. The repair doesn't wipe your data, but a backup is always smart.
  3. The phone is moved to the new frame. Because the new screen comes with its frame, your battery, board and cameras are carefully moved across into it.
  4. The back is sealed up and the phone is put back together.
  5. Everything is tested. Touch across the whole screen, the fingerprint reader, cameras, speakers, charging and buttons are all checked before you get it back.

Your photos, apps and settings stay on the phone. You walk out with the same phone and a brand-new genuine screen.

How to back up your Galaxy before the repair

The repair itself doesn't wipe your phone, but backing up is always worth the five minutes, especially when a screen is already failing. If your screen still works, here are the easiest ways:

  • Samsung Cloud / Smart Switch. Go to Settings → Accounts and backup and back up your data. Smart Switch on a computer can save a full copy too.
  • Google account. Make sure Google backup is switched on so your contacts, apps and settings are saved.
  • Photos. Check that Google Photos or OneDrive has finished uploading. Many people think their photos are backed up when the sync stopped months ago.
  • Messaging apps. WhatsApp and similar apps have their own backup inside their settings. Run it before you come in.

If part of the screen is already black and you can't reach these settings, bring the phone in anyway. A working display is fitted first, so in most cases you'll get full access to your data again once the repair is done.

Is it worth fixing an older Galaxy S phone?

This is a fair question, because a genuine S series screen isn't the cheapest repair. Here's how we'd think about it:

  • If the rest of the phone is good (battery holds up, cameras work, it's still fast), a new genuine screen gives you a phone that looks and feels new again, usually for a fraction of the price of a new flagship.
  • If the phone still gets updates from Samsung, it has plenty of life left in it.
  • If the battery is also tired, ask about doing both at once. The phone is already open, so it's the sensible time to do it.

Ask for the quote first, compare it with what a new phone would cost you, and decide from there. A good store will give you the honest numbers and let you choose.

Looking after your new screen

Once you've got a fresh genuine screen, a few simple things help it last:

  • Use a decent case with a raised lip around the screen, so it doesn't land face-first on hard floors.
  • Choose a screen protector made for your model that works with the under-display fingerprint reader. Thick, cheap glass protectors can stop it from reading properly.
  • Keep it out of back pockets. Sitting on your phone is one of the most common causes of panel damage we see.
  • Avoid heat. Don't leave it on the car dashboard or in direct sun for long periods.

Don't wait too long

It's tempting to live with one thin line, and plenty of people do for a while. The problem is it rarely stays that way:

  • A single line often spreads into several lines, or the area around it turns black.
  • Parts of the screen can stop responding to touch, which makes the phone hard to use.
  • If the display dies completely, it's much harder to back up your photos and data.
  • If there's a crack as well, dust and moisture can get inside and damage other parts.

If the phone is still working, now is the best time to back it up and book it in.

You updated your iPhone last night. This morning it was on 100%, and by lunch it's already asking for low power mode. Sound familiar?

Every time Apple releases a big iOS update, the same thing happens at the repair counter. People come in holding their phone and asking one question: “Is my battery dying, or is it the update?” It's a fair question, and the answer decides whether you need a repair at all.

The good news is you can work it out yourself in about a minute. This guide walks you through it the same way we would at the counter: what to check, what's normal, what isn't, and what to do about it without wasting money.

Why the battery drains faster right after an update

An iOS update isn't just a new version sitting on your phone. For the first few days, your iPhone is busy behind the scenes:

  • Re-indexing everything. Your photos, messages, files and apps get scanned again so search and the Photos app work properly.
  • Updating apps. A lot of apps push their own updates straight after a new iOS release, and they all download and install in the background.
  • Re-learning your habits. Features like Optimised Battery Charging have to learn your routine again.

All of that uses power, so a bit of extra drain for the first few days is normal. It doesn't mean anything is broken.

Sometimes an update also has its own bug that makes the battery drain more than it should. When that happens, lots of people notice at the same time, and Apple usually fixes it in the next small update.

So the update can explain the drain. What it can't do is wear out your battery overnight. That's why the next step matters.

Step 1: Check your battery health

This is the one check that tells you almost everything. On your iPhone go to:

Settings → Battery → Battery Health (on some versions it's Battery Health & Charging)

Look at the number next to Maximum Capacity. It shows how much charge your battery can hold now compared with when it was new.

If your battery health is good (around 85% or higher)

Your battery is fine. The drain is coming from the update, not from worn hardware. In this case:

  • Give it a few days. Most post-update drain settles down by itself once the phone finishes its background work.
  • Keep iOS up to date. If the update itself has a battery bug, the next update usually fixes it.
  • You don't need a repair. Replacing a healthy battery won't make an update bug go away, and we'd tell you the same thing in store.

If your battery health is low (around 80% or below)

This is a different story. A battery at 80% or less has lost a real chunk of its capacity, and phones in this range usually feel noticeably worse. Often the battery was already worn before the update, and the extra background work after updating just made it obvious.

Waiting for the next update won't help here. Software can't put capacity back into a worn battery. The fix is a new battery.

If it says “Service”

If your iPhone shows a Service message under Battery Health, the phone itself has decided the battery needs replacing. You'll often see sudden shutdowns or the phone slowing down to protect itself. Don't ignore this one. A battery replacement sorts it out.

Step 2: Look for the signs of a worn battery

Battery health is the main clue, but how the phone behaves matters too. These are the things we hear most often from people whose battery is genuinely worn out:

  • It shuts off with charge left. The phone dies at 15%, 20% or even 30%, sometimes in the cold or when you open the camera.
  • The percentage jumps around. It shows 40%, then suddenly 12%, or it sits on 1% for ages.
  • It gets warm doing nothing much. Scrolling or messaging shouldn't make the phone hot.
  • It feels slow. When a battery can't deliver enough power, iOS can slow the phone down to stop it shutting off.
  • It was slipping before you updated. If you were already charging twice a day last month, the update didn't cause it.
  • It needs the charger by mid-afternoon on a normal day, when it used to last until night.

One or two of these together with low battery health is a pretty clear answer: it's the battery, not the update.

Don't buy a new phone because of the battery

This is the bit we really want people to hear. We regularly see customers who were about to spend well over a thousand dollars on a new iPhone because “the old one won't last the day”. When we check it, the screen is fine, the cameras are fine, the phone is still fast, and the only worn part is the battery.

A battery is a consumable part. Like tyres on a car, it wears out with use. Replacing it doesn't mean your phone is old or finished. It just means it's done a lot of charging.

  • A battery replacement starts from $69 at listed stores.
  • It usually takes about 30–45 minutes, so you can wait for it or grab a coffee.
  • Your photos, apps and settings stay exactly where they are.

Our advice is simple. Try a new battery first. If your phone feels like new again, you've saved yourself the price of a new phone. If you still want to upgrade later, you've lost very little, and a phone with a fresh battery is worth more when you sell or trade it in.

What happens when you get the battery replaced

If you've never had a phone repaired before, here's what to expect so there are no surprises:

  1. Quick check first. The technician looks at your battery health and makes sure nothing else is going on, like a charging port problem or liquid damage, so you don't pay for the wrong fix.
  2. The screen comes off carefully. On an iPhone, the battery sits under the display. The screen is opened with proper tools and heat so it isn't damaged.
  3. The old battery comes out. It's held in with adhesive strips, and a good technician removes it without bending or puncturing it.
  4. The new battery goes in with fresh adhesive, and the screen is sealed back down.
  5. Everything gets tested. Charging, the screen, Face ID, cameras and buttons are all checked before it's handed back.

About the “unknown part” message

On newer iPhones, you might see a message saying the battery couldn't be verified as genuine after a replacement. This is Apple's parts-pairing system. It doesn't stop the battery from working, and your phone will charge and run normally. The main difference is that the battery health percentage may not be shown in Settings. Ask the store about this before the repair so you know what to expect for your model.

Settings that help while you wait for the update to settle

If your battery health is good and you're just riding out the first few days after an update, these settings can take the edge off without changing how you use your phone:

  • Low Power Mode. Swipe down to Control Centre and turn it on when you need the battery to last. It pauses background activity, which is exactly what's draining it after an update.
  • Check Settings → Battery. The list shows which apps are using the most power. If one app is way out in front, update it or restart it.
  • Leave it on charge overnight on Wi-Fi. That gives the phone time to finish its background work while it's plugged in, instead of during your day.
  • Restart the phone once. A simple restart after an update clears out anything that got stuck.

None of this will fix a worn battery, but if the battery is healthy, it helps you get through the settling-in period.

Is it worth replacing the battery on an older iPhone?

In most cases, yes. Here's how to think about it:

  • Does your iPhone still get iOS updates? If it does, it has plenty of life left, and a new battery makes it feel close to new again.
  • Is the rest of the phone in good shape? If the screen, cameras and charging port all work, the battery is usually the only thing holding it back.
  • Is the screen cracked too? Ask about doing both in one visit. It's quicker, and you walk out with a phone that looks and works like new.

If your iPhone no longer gets updates at all, a battery can still keep it going as a backup phone or for a family member. Just know that the apps you use may stop supporting it over time.

How to keep a new battery healthy for longer

Once you've got a fresh battery, a few easy habits help it last:

  • Leave Optimised Battery Charging on. It stops the phone sitting at 100% all night.
  • Avoid heat. Heat is a battery's worst enemy. Don't leave your phone on the car dashboard or charge it under a pillow.
  • Use a decent charger and cable. Very cheap chargers can deliver messy power and make the phone run hot.
  • Don't stress about small top-ups. Charging from 40% to 80% is perfectly fine. You don't need to run it flat first.
  • Take a thick case off if the phone gets hot while fast charging.

When the drain isn't the battery or the update

Occasionally, the battery is healthy, the update has settled, and the phone still drains fast. That usually points to something else:

  • The phone has been dropped or got wet. Damage on the board can cause a small constant drain. It doesn't always show straight away. See water damage repair.
  • It drains while charging, or charges very slowly. That's often the cable, the adapter or the charging port. Our guide on a phone that's charging but the percentage isn't going up walks through it.
  • It's always hot, even when idle. Constant heat with a healthy battery is worth getting checked properly in store.

In these cases, a proper diagnosis comes first. A good repairer will test it and tell you what's wrong before charging you for parts.

Quick checklist

  1. Go to Settings → Battery → Battery Health.
  2. 85% or higher: give it a few days and keep iOS updated. No repair needed.
  3. 80% or lower, or “Service”: the battery is worn. Replace it, usually in 30–45 minutes.
  4. Thinking about a new phone? Try a new battery first. It costs far less.
  5. Healthy battery but still draining? Get it checked for charging or board faults.

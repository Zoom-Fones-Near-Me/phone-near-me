It usually starts on the lock screen. You pick up your phone and the passcode dots are filling in by themselves. One, two, three, wrong code. Then it starts again. A minute later it's opening apps, tapping random spots, scrolling and typing things you never meant to type.

This is called ghost touch. The touchscreen is registering taps that nobody is making. It's one of the more stressful faults we see, because the phone isn't just annoying to use. It can lock you out of your own phone.

This guide explains what causes ghost touch, the one thing you can try at home, why the passcode screen is the real danger, and how it gets fixed.

What ghost touch looks like

From what we see at the counter, it nearly always follows the same pattern:

  • It types the passcode by itself. This is the most common first sign. The phone sits on the lock screen and the code gets entered over and over.
  • Then the random tapping starts. Apps open, keyboards pop up, settings change, the screen scrolls on its own.
  • Your own touches stop working properly. You tap one thing and the phone does another, or ignores you completely.
  • It comes and goes. It can be fine for an hour, then go wild again. That makes people think it's a software glitch, but it usually isn't.

The real danger: getting locked out

This is the part most people don't expect. Every phone limits how many wrong passcodes it accepts. When ghost touch keeps hitting the lock screen, it burns through those attempts for you.

On an iPhone, each run of wrong codes makes it wait longer before you can try again, and after too many it shows iPhone Unavailable or Security Lockout. At that point the only way back in is to erase the phone. If you don't have a recent backup, your photos, messages and everything else on the phone are gone. If you've switched on Erase Data in your passcode settings, the phone wipes itself after 10 wrong attempts.

We've seen a lot of people get locked out exactly like this. Most of them would have kept their data if they'd brought the phone in a day earlier.

If your phone is typing its own passcode, stop using it and get professional help straight away. Don't wait to see if it settles down.

What causes it

Ghost touch shows up on every brand: iPhone, Samsung, Google Pixel, Oppo and the rest. We see it most on LCD screens, but OLED screens get it too. In nearly every case it comes down to one of three things:

  • A cheap aftermarket screen. This is the big one. If your screen was replaced at some point with a low-cost copy, the touch layer is often poor quality and can start sending false taps weeks or months later.
  • A faulty screen. Sometimes the touch layer in the screen just fails, whether it's an original screen or a replacement. A drop or pressure on the phone can start it off.
  • Liquid. Water, coffee or even a lot of moisture inside the phone can make the touch layer act up. It doesn't always happen on the day the phone got wet. See water damage repair if the phone has been wet.

Very rarely the problem is on the main board rather than the screen. A good technician will test with a known-good screen first to tell the two apart.

What you can try at home

Honestly, not much. Ghost touch is almost always hardware, so there's no setting that fixes it.

  • Restart the phone. It's worth one try. If it was a one-off glitch, a restart clears it.
  • Keep the screen off. If it's still happening after a restart, press the side button to turn the screen off and leave it off. A dark screen can't tap in passcodes.
  • Don't keep unlocking it to check. Every time the screen wakes up, the ghost touches get another chance at your passcode.

If restarting doesn't fix it, the screen needs looking at.

How it's repaired

In most cases the fix is replacing the screen. On modern phones the glass, touch layer and display are bonded together into one panel, so they're replaced as one part. Once a good-quality screen is in, the false taps stop.

If the phone has had liquid in it, the technician will also check the inside for corrosion, because that can come back to bite later. In the rare case where the main board is at fault, it needs a board repair instead.

A screen repair doesn't erase your phone. If the phone isn't locked out yet, your data stays exactly as it was. That's another reason not to wait.

One tip: if your ghost touch started after an earlier cheap screen repair, don't just fit another cheap screen. Ask for a better-quality part. Otherwise you may be back in a few months with the same problem.

The doctor who called everyone back

We'll finish with our favourite ghost touch story. A doctor who trains at the gym in our centre spilled something on their phone during a busy day. They were in theatre and didn't have time to look at it properly.

By the time they came out, the ghost touches had gone through the recent calls list and called back every single number in it. Then the people started calling back to see what was wrong, and the doctor couldn't answer them, because the touchscreen wouldn't respond to a real finger.

They spent the next week explaining to half their contacts why they'd been called. We had a good laugh about it together when they came in, and they still bring it up when we chat. The lesson: when a phone starts touching itself, deal with it straight away.

Quick checklist

  1. Restart the phone once.
  2. Still happening? Turn the screen off and stop trying to unlock it.
  3. If it's typing your passcode, get help today. Too many wrong codes can mean erasing the phone.
  4. Get the screen replaced with a good-quality part. See screen repair.

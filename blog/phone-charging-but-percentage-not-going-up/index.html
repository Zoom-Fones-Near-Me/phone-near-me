Phone Charging but the Percentage Not Going Up? Here's Why

Phone plugged in but the battery % won't climb? A technician's step-by-step guide: cable, adapter, charging port or battery, and how long each fix takes.

You plug your phone in, the charging icon comes on… and the battery percentage barely moves. Or it creeps up 1% an hour. Or it actually goes down while plugged in. It's one of the most common things we see at the repair bench, and the good news is that most of the time the fix is quick and cheap.

Here's how a repair technician works through it, starting with the checks you can do at home for free.

Step 1: Suspect the cable and adapter first

In most cases, the phone isn't the problem at all — it's the cable or the power adapter. Cables wear out internally long before they look damaged, and cheap or old adapters often can't deliver enough power to charge a modern phone while it's in use.

Car chargers are the biggest culprit. Many car USB ports and plug-in car chargers put out so little power that the phone shows the charging icon but gains almost nothing — especially with maps or music running. Don't judge your phone by how it charges in the car.

Step 2: Swap things around to find the faulty part

The quickest way to find the problem is to test each piece separately:

  • Charge a different device with the same cable and adapter. If the other device also charges slowly, your cable or adapter is the problem — replace it.
  • Charge your phone with a different cable and adapter that you know works well, plugged into a wall socket (not the car, not a laptop).
  • Try wireless charging if you have a wireless charger at home and your phone supports it. If your phone charges normally on a wireless pad but not through the cable, the charging port is the most likely fault.

Step 3: If it's none of those, it's usually the battery

If you've ruled out the cable, adapter and port and the percentage still won't climb, the most common cause is the battery itself. An aged or damaged battery can stop accepting charge properly, so the phone draws power but the percentage doesn't move the way it should.

The good news: a battery replacement is one of the quickest and cheapest repairs there is.

“It just happened out of nowhere”

We hear this a lot. But very often, the phone was dropped at some point, or had contact with liquid the owner didn't notice — a damp pocket, a splash, a bathroom. The damage doesn't always show straight away. It can take days or weeks before charging starts to misbehave, so it feels sudden even though it isn't.

If your phone's back glass is cracked, the impact that broke it may have damaged the battery underneath too.

“Liquid detected” or moisture warning on your phone?

Both iPhone and Samsung phones now show a warning when they detect liquid or moisture in the charging port, and they may stop or limit charging through the cable until it clears.

When the warning won't go away, the port usually needs to be taken apart and properly cleaned, or replaced — that's a charging port repair, not a do-it-yourself job.

Be aware that liquid damage is tricky. It's unpredictable and almost anything can be affected. After heavy liquid exposure, the phone may point to the charging port, but the real fault is often on the motherboard. That's why a proper diagnosis matters before paying for a part that won't fix it — see water damage repair.

How a repair shop actually diagnoses it

At the shop, guessing isn't good enough. Technicians use specialised chargers with built-in ammeters. These filtered chargers remove electrical noise so you can see exactly how much current (amps) the phone is drawing and at what voltage, moment by moment. Those readings tell the technician whether the fault is in the battery, the charging port or the phone's board — before anything is opened up.

The less common causes

  • Motherboard (logic board) fault — less common, and usually the result of a drop or liquid damage. See water damage repair if your phone may have got wet.
  • NFC / wireless charging antenna fault — rare, but it does happen, and it's one of the things the diagnostic tests check for.

How long does the repair take?

  • Battery or charging port: usually fixed the same day, typically in 30–45 minutes, and it's an affordable repair — battery replacements start from $69 and charging port repairs from $79 at listed stores.
  • Motherboard repair: usually takes 3–5 business days.

Quick checklist

  1. Stop testing with a car charger — use a wall socket.
  2. Try your cable and adapter on another device.
  3. Try a different, known-good cable and adapter on your phone.
  4. Try wireless charging if you can.
  5. Still not charging properly? It's most likely the battery or the charging port — both usually fixed in under an hour.
